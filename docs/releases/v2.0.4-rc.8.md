### What changed in rc.8

Tested on a real WE router (Huawei HG630 V2, VDSL). rc.7 found the router and read the
line state, the internet session and the router's uptime, but not the line speed. rc.8
reads what that router actually offers:

- **Line speed (sync):** routers that leave it empty in the usual place (like the HG630
  V2) report it with the internet connection. The router card now shows it.
- **Why the last disconnect happened:** the router records the reason for the last
  drop. When it says the provider ended the session ("ISP disconnect") or stopped
  answering, the router card says so, the outage row in the report says "the provider
  ended the internet session", and "What your data says" counts those drops with a
  button to file a complaint.
- Actions a router doesn't support (like the traffic counters on the HG630 V2) are asked
  once and then skipped, instead of every minute.

**The router's own page (optional, with its password)**

Over UPnP the HG630 V2 gives no noise margin, no attenuation and no traffic counters.
Its own web page has all three. Settings → App → "Read the router's status" now takes
the router's user name (default `admin`), password and, if it isn't 192.168.1.1, its
address. With a password set, the app signs in to the router's page every 5 minutes
(more often on lines faster than about 57 Mbps, so the 4 GB counters can't wrap twice
between reads), reads the line and the counters, and signs out.

- The router card adds the noise margin (SNR) and attenuation, both directions, the
  speed the line could reach, and how long the line has been in sync.
- Data-plan tracking works on this router now, from its own counters.
- An outage during which the line resynced (its "in sync for" time started over) is
  marked as "the line to the provider was down", even if the router never reported the
  line as down.
- "What your data says" warns when the noise margin over the last three days is below
  6 dB, which makes a DSL line drop easily. That's measured on the provider's side of
  the line and is a strong point for a complaint.
- Safety: the password is only ever sent to an address on your home network. If the
  router rejects it, the app stops trying until you change it, so it can't lock you out
  of your router. "Test the router password" checks it once and shows the line speed
  and SNR.
- The password is stored in `config.json` like the Telegram token, and never shown in the
  dashboard after saving. The privacy policy says so.

**Tutorial video**

A narrated tour of the Settings page (about 7 minutes, English voice) explains every
setting, section by section: what it does and why you'd change it.

- Open it with "Watch the settings tour" at the top of Settings, or "Settings tour" in the
  sidebar next to FAQ and What's new.
- Subtitles in English, Arabic, French, Turkish and Urdu come with the app; the one for the
  app's language is on by default.
- The video itself isn't inside the app. It's a file on this release and streams from
  GitHub only when you press play. If it can't load (offline, or not published yet), the
  page says so and offers to try again. The privacy policy lists it.

### How to test

- [ ] With UPnP on, the router card shows "Line speed (sync)" after a minute.
- [ ] Settings → App: enter the router password → "Test the router password": a message
      with the line speed and SNR. Save. Within 5 minutes the router card shows SNR,
      attenuation and "Line in sync for".
- [ ] Enter a wrong password and save: the card says the router rejected it, and the
      router's own page still lets you log in (the app tried only once).
- [ ] Complaints → line details: set the data limit. The router card shows usage since the
      renewal day.
- [ ] After the provider drops the connection (or after unplugging the phone line for a
      minute), the card shows "Last disconnect". The next day's report row for that
      outage says who ended it.
- [ ] Settings → "Watch the settings tour": the video plays with the subtitles in the app's
      language, and the subtitle menu offers all five languages. With the internet off,
      pressing play shows a message with "Try again".

### Known limits

- Reading the router's page is built and tested for the Huawei HG630 V2 (and should work
  on Huawei HG633/HG659/DG8045, which share the same page). Other routers show only the
  UPnP details; the card says so.
- The router's page counters are 32-bit. Reads are spaced so they wrap at most once, but
  if the app is closed for a while traffic over 4 GB in that time can't be counted.
- While the app reads the router's page (a few seconds every few minutes), signing in to
  the router yourself may say someone else is signed in; try again a moment later.
- The last-disconnect reason is the router's own record of the latest drop only; older
  drops are judged from the line and session state the app saw at the time.
