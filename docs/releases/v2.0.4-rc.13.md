### What changed in rc.13

**Video tours**

The single Settings video is now one short narrated video for each page of the app,
English voice, subtitles in English, Arabic, French, Turkish and Urdu:

- Overview: the live status, the four numbers, the outage timeline, what your data says,
  the router card (line, sync speed, noise margin, data used), response time, recent events,
  availability and check targets.
- History: picking a day, filters, Excel/CSV export, the day's totals, and how to read the
  duration and cause of each event.
- Notification history: where each alert went, what was held back and why (quiet hours,
  a channel turned off, a short outage), the filters and "Mark all as read".
- Speed test: running a test, the result and the alert level, lag under load (grades A+
  to F and how to fix a poor one), today's tests and automatic tests.
- Complaints: one complaint from start to finish: your line details, the last 7 days,
  logging the complaint with its number, the provider's deadline, before and since, the
  ready-made text, the evidence ZIP, escalating to the regulator and closing it.
- Monthly report: every section, what the router and route lines under a cause mean,
  saving it as a PDF and downloading the evidence pack.
- Settings: the existing tour, with its last scene updated.

Every page has a small "How this page works" button at the top that opens its own video.
The sidebar link is now "Video tours": a gallery with each video's poster, title, length
and a one-line description, in the app's language. `/tutorial?v=<id>` opens one directly.
The videos are files on this release (`internet-monitor-tour-<id>.mp4`) and stream from
GitHub only when you press play; subtitles and posters come with the app. The website's
video page shows the same seven videos, and still plays the Settings video from older
releases that only have `internet-monitor-tutorial.mp4`.

**Plain words everywhere**

The app and the website were read through for words only a technician would know, in all
five languages. The two desktop versions are now "runs in the background" and "opens like a
regular program" instead of tray/window; "tray icon" is "the small icon next to the clock";
Latency, Jitter and Packet loss are Response time, Fluctuation and Data loss; terms that
stay because you'll meet them in a router or a setting (DNS, webhook, UPnP, SQM/QoS) get a
one-line plain explanation. The complaint text sent to your provider is unchanged.

**Website: a simpler download page and a picture guide**

- The download page recognises your computer and shows one big "Download for Windows" (or
  Mac, Linux) button with its size, the regular-program version as a smaller choice under it,
  and four short steps. Other systems are under "Using a different device?", and Raspberry
  Pi, file names and checksums under "Advanced". On a Mac it picks Apple chip or Intel when
  the browser says which; on a phone it says to open the page on your computer. Without
  JavaScript it is a simple four-way chooser.
- The beginner's guide is six steps, each one sentence and one picture: real screenshots of
  the app in your language and theme, with the place to tap highlighted. The Raspberry Pi
  part moved to the download page's Advanced section. `site/tools/guide-shots.cjs`
  regenerates the screenshots from sample data.

### How to test

- [ ] On each of the seven pages, "How this page works" opens Video tours with that page's
      video selected; it plays with the subtitles in the app's language, and the subtitle
      menu offers all five. Switch the app to Arabic: the gallery and the buttons read right
      to left. With the internet off, pressing play shows a message with "Try again".
- [ ] After this release is published, the website's Videos page plays each of the seven
      videos with subtitles.

- [ ] Open https://khaymah.github.io/internet-monitor/ar/download/ on Windows, a Mac and a
      phone: each shows the right big button or message; the guide's pictures follow the
      light/dark switch.
- [ ] The app's cards read Response time, Fluctuation and Data loss in every language.

### Known limits

- Mac and Linux still need the one-time "allow it to run" step (`chmod +x`), now tucked under
  "Show how"; a Mac installer would remove it.

- The narration is a synthetic English voice; the French, Turkish and Urdu subtitles are
  machine translations that still need a native speaker's check.
- The videos show sample data, so the numbers in them won't match yours.
