### What changed in rc.3

**Alerts on your phone, and to any service**

- **ntfy push notifications.** Install the free ntfy app, subscribe to a topic with
  a hard-to-guess name, and paste its address in Settings → Alerts. Outages arrive
  as high-priority pushes; the weekly summary and speed results arrive quietly. Works
  with ntfy.sh or your own ntfy server, with an optional access token. ntfy has its
  own column in Settings → Notifications.
- **Webhook to any service.** The webhook field still formats messages for Discord and
  Slack, and now accepts any other https address too (or http on your own network).
  Those get a simple JSON message (`source`, `type`, `title`, `text`, `time`, `data`)
  that Home Assistant, n8n, Zapier, IFTTT and similar tools can use.

**Lag under load (bufferbloat)**

- The speed test now measures your ping while the line is idle and again while the
  download and upload are running. How much the ping rises gives a grade from A+ to F,
  with a plain explanation: "calls and games stay smooth" or "heavy lag while the
  line is busy, turn on SQM/QoS on the router". The grade also shows in the speed
  history and in the Discord/Slack/Telegram speed message.

**Where the connection broke**

- When an outage starts, the app traces the route to the internet with the
  system's own tool (`tracert` on Windows, `traceroute` on macOS/Linux) and saves it.
  For each outage, the monthly report then says:
  - nothing answered, not even your router: check the router, its power and the cable,
  - your router answered but nothing beyond it: the break was at the provider,
  - or the hop inside the provider's network where it stopped.
- The traces are in the evidence ZIP (`logs/traceroute_*`).

**Small fixes**

- The download table in the release notes is no longer indented.

### How to test

- [ ] Settings → Alerts → ntfy: paste `https://ntfy.sh/<your-topic>` → "Send test": the
      phone gets the test. Unplug the router: the phone gets an outage push once the
      connection is back. Check the Notifications history shows "ntfy".
- [ ] Turn off the ntfy column for "Unstable" in Settings → Notifications: unstable
      alerts stop reaching the phone and the history says why.
- [ ] Webhook: paste a https://webhook.site address → "Send test": it receives JSON with
      `source: "internet-monitor"`. Discord and Slack addresses still get the formatted
      message.
- [ ] Run a speed test: a grade card appears under the gauge, with the idle and loaded
      ping. Start a big download in another program during the test: the grade gets worse.
- [ ] Unplug the router's internet cable (keep the PC connected to the router) for a
      minute: the next monthly report says your router answered but nothing beyond it.
      Unplug the PC's own cable instead: it says nothing answered.
- [ ] Download the evidence ZIP for this month: it has a `logs/traceroute_*` file.

### Known limits

- The route is traced once the app decides the line is down (about 15 seconds in), and
  at most once every 10 minutes. A very short drop can be over before the trace starts.
- Some Linux systems don't have `traceroute` or `tracepath` installed. There the trace
  is skipped and the app log says so.
- ntfy and webhook alerts can only leave the computer once the internet is back, so an
  outage arrives when the connection returns. This is true of every channel except
  the desktop alert.
